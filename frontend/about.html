Government Document Monitoring

Regulatory Documents & Captured Changes

JurisMon monitors selected public government sources and compares captured document versions. Its catalogue includes US city and county portals alongside international national registries. Search results reflect the documents successfully captured, and are not limited to municipal zoning.

Coverage and exclusions

The catalogue combines selected US city and county portals with international national registries. It is not complete coverage of any jurisdiction.

Configured sources

A source is configured when its endpoint is listed for crawl attempts. A listed endpoint does not mean every document or legal change is available.

Operational status

Health labels reflect recorded source checks. They do not prove that a recent crawl stored usable legal text. CAPTCHA-protected, login-only and unreachable pages may be excluded; access restrictions are not bypassed.

Captured documents

Search shows successfully stored captures and comparisons. Some historical captures contain portal boilerplate or incomplete text while extraction repairs continue. Open the official source to verify findings.

History begins with captures actually collected. Capture dates are not legal effective dates. Missing crawl intervals and documents never captured cannot be reconstructed. Differences can reflect page formatting or metadata rather than a legal amendment. JurisMon does not certify completeness or provide legal advice.

1 Where the Data Begins: Continuous Multi-Portal Tracking

Regulatory changes are fragmented across hundreds of disconnected city websites, unindexed PDF minutes, and legacy government portals. JurisMon unifies this fragmented landscape at the source:

Platform Networks

The crawler includes adapters for Municode, Granicus Legistar, CivicPlus, XML/Atom and REST APIs. Adapter support does not guarantee that every configured portal is currently reachable or producing documents.

Official Primary Sources

Scheduled crawl attempts retrieve documents from configured public sources. Availability, access restrictions and extraction quality vary by source.

Jurisdictional Scope

Coverage includes local US government portals and national legislative registries outside the US. Some configured sources are blocked, unreachable or awaiting repair; inclusion does not establish complete jurisdiction coverage.

3 The Core Backbone: Historical Retention & Legal Precedent

COMPETITIVE MOAT

Why Immutable Version Control is Our Competitive Moat

In land-use litigation, entitlement disputes, and real estate acquisitions, knowing current law is only half the battle. Municipalities routinely overwrite web pages and update code portals without preserving public changelogs, wiping out historical record accessibility. JurisMon retains captured snapshots for comparison. Capture timestamps identify when a document was collected, not its legal effective date.

Point-in-Time Reconstruction

Compare document versions captured since monitoring began. JurisMon cannot reconstruct versions it never captured or certify the law in force on a permit date.

Immutable Snapshot Vault

Successfully stored snapshots are timestamped. Gaps in crawling or extraction can leave gaps in the captured history.

Complete Statutory Lineage

Review changes between captured versions and follow the available original source links. A captured change is not a guarantee of complete statutory lineage.

5 What Changes: Section-Level (§) Statutory Delta Analysis

JurisMon does not force you to manually read through 180-page meeting agendas. The diff engine compares successive captured versions using section (§), paragraph and word-level analysis where the extracted structure permits:

+ ADDED

Added Clauses (§)

Highlights added text between captured versions. Changes may include zoning restrictions, setbacks or definitions, depending on the source.

− REMOVED

Removed Language

Explicit tracking of deleted statutory sections, repealed paragraphs, or removed conditional-use rights.

Δ MODIFIED

Modified Parameters

Word-for-word comparative analysis surfacing numerical shifts (e.g., setback updated from 20 ft to 15 ft).
